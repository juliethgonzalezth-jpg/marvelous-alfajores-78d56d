Role profile & tactical fit

scout view

Role profile

  • Modern full-back who defends the wide channel first and joins the attack second.
  • Deep, wide outlet in goalkeeper build-up; receives on the back foot and plays forward early.
  • Progresses the ball through carries into the opponent's half when the team chases the game.
  • Dead-ball option: long-range striking and free-kick delivery documented in club footage.
  • Centre-back background gives him aerial presence and duel comfort for a full-back.

Preferred environments

  • Back four with a full-back asked to run the flank for 90 minutes.
  • Possession teams that use the full-back as the wide build-up outlet.
  • Transition-oriented systems that reward repeat sprints and recovery runs.
  • Back three / five as a right wing-back, given his engine, or as the right centre-back.

Less suited to

  • An inverted full-back role as the team's primary creator between the lines. His game is vertical, not a half-space playmaker.
  • A squad with no Spanish-speaking staff and no integration plan: first move abroad, first professional environment.

Strengths & development areas

scout view

Key strengths

  • Physical output that sets the ceiling. Top PlayerLoad and top-four explosive efforts in the squad against a Bundesliga academy, on the fifth match of a four-week tour. Full-backs who can repeat that every three days are scarce at 18.
  • Recovery running. On both Hertha goals he is the defender arriving at the far post at full sprint. It is the habit that keeps full-backs in professional teams.
  • Ball striking from a defensive position. Long-range shooting and free-kick delivery give a coach a set-piece option from the back line.
  • Positional range. Genuine cover at right centre-back and as a defensive midfielder, which matters for a first-team squad with 20 outfield players.
  • Character and leadership. Captaincy history, visible communication, and a documented return from surgery without shortcuts.

Development areas with context

  • Far-post space in defensive transitions. Positioning is correct once set; the development is the first two steps after a central turnover, so that the far-post space is closed before the cross rather than on it.
  • Final action after the overlap. The run arrives; the choice between an early cross, a cut-back and a recycle is still made a beat late. Improves with volume of attacking repetitions.
  • Playing forward through pressure. Under a well-organised press his first choice is the safe ball. A senior environment with a clear build-up model will speed his scanning.
  • Weak-foot use and left-side crossing remain unproven at this level.
  • Exposure. Six international friendlies is a small sample. The first six months in Europe should be treated as an evaluation period on both sides.

Role reference in the current market

The closest professional archetype is the high-output right-back who defends first and adds set-piece and long-range value, as opposed to the inverted, half-space creator. Clubs that run a back four with high, wide full-backs and a possession base, or a back five with running wing-backs, use this profile most.

This is a role comparison, not a level comparison. FUTBOOM does not attach a player name because comparisons of that kind rarely help a sporting director and never survive a first training session.

Recommended next environment

  • A first team in a European second tier, or a top-flight U21/B team in a club with a real first-team pathway.
  • Playing time in the plan: a clear route to 1,500 minutes in the first season.
  • Spanish-speaking staff or players and structured language support for the first six months.
  • Strength and conditioning staff who continue the load–velocity work already started.
